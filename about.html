A little context

Build the thing.
Build the place to learn it.

I'm a Cuban-American engineer and educator from Miami. These days, I lead the Roberto C. Goizueta Innovation Center at Belen Jesuit, teach engineering and AP Physics C, and coach students through robotics and fabrication projects.

My route here was not a straight line. I studied biomedical engineering, worked in research, and eventually found that I wanted to bring engineering to more people through teaching. Robotics helped me reconnect with the kind of hands-on problem-solving that first drew me to engineering. Since then, I've worked to make that experience more available to students and the wider community.

At Belen, I helped grow a small 3D-printing program into a 9,000-square-foot K-12 fabrication and innovation center. At Moonlighter FabLab, I teach digital fabrication to learners of different ages and experience levels. In both places, the point is the same: give people good tools, a real problem, and room to learn by doing.

I want people to know how to make things so they can make the world a little better.

Outside the lab, I'm usually reading, building, cooking, or following some odd idea that caught my attention. Strong coffee and freshly baked bread are reliable parts of the process.
