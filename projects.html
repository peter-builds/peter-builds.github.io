01 / ROBOTICS

Fab Academy / 2024

DAP-R: a desktop robot with a little personality

DAP-R began as a modular robotic companion. Through the Fab Academy build, the idea became a desktop robot with a moving head, expressive screen eyes, a sensing system, and an interchangeable laser-cut bowtie.

The design changed as the engineering got real. I moved from a mobile concept to a stable desktop form, tested the motor and servo assemblies separately, and simplified the final scope to focus on interaction. Sensor and I2C issues led to a change in sensing approach; repeated board revisions made testing more observable and reliable.

Built with
RP2040, screen, distance sensor, motor, servo
Made using
3D printing, laser cutting, custom electronics
Documentation
Design, fabrication, code, testing, and files

02 / EDUCATION

Belen Jesuit / Program development

From two printers to a school-wide innovation center

A fabrication program can be more than a room of machines. I helped grow a two-printer maker program into a 9,000-square-foot K-12 fabrication and innovation center, combining digital tools with curriculum, operations, safety, and teacher development.

The work spans CNC machining, laser cutting, 3D printing, embedded systems, CAD, robotics, and project-based courses. It also means making the program usable: aligning work to learning goals, training faculty, building partnerships, managing equipment, and creating pathways for students with different levels of experience.

Scope
K-12 fabrication and innovation
Practice
Curriculum, operations, safety, and mentoring
Tools
CNC, laser, additive, electronics, CAD
03 / BIOMEDICAL

University of Miami / Research engineering

A six-well electrode for cell research

While working in a biomedical research lab, I built an electrical bioreactor using an Arduino microcontroller and analog circuits. One outcome was a six-well plate electrode designed to stimulate six cell cultures at once.

This early project sits at the intersection of my engineering training and my interest in building practical tools for people doing careful work. The lab notebook mattered as much as the circuit: documenting the design and experiments made the system easier to understand, repeat, and improve.

Application
Electrical stimulation of cell cultures
System
Arduino control and analog circuitry
Outcome
Parallel stimulation across six wells
